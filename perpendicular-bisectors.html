Geometry Through Adinkra: Perpendicular Bisectors

Accessible geometry enrichment for secondary learners, including students with SLD and autism.

Learning target

SWBAT: Identify a perpendicular bisector, find equal segment lengths, and solve a midpoint equation.

Success: At least 8 correct out of 10 questions.

Remember: Perpendicular = 90°. Bisector = two equal parts. Both must be true.

See the geometry

AM = MB = 8. The vertical line crosses at 90°.

Akan culture and Twi connection

Nkyimu: An Adinkra design term referring to crossed divisions made in cloth before printing. It is associated with precision and skill. Look for straight lines, intersections, and repeated spacing in geometric patterns.

Nkyinkyim: “Twisting,” an Adinkra concept about adaptability. It reminds us that learning can involve trying a different method.

Sankofa: Returning to retrieve something valuable from the past. In mathematics, reviewing an earlier step can help us correct a mistake.

These are Akan cultural and Twi-language connections, not translations of the technical geometry terms. Not every Adinkra pattern contains a perpendicular bisector.

References: CSDT Adinkra collection · Nkyinkyim meaning

I Do — Worked example

AB = 18 cm. M is the midpoint. Find AM.

1. Find the whole: 18 cm.
2. Divide by 2: 18 ÷ 2 = 9.
3. Answer: AM = 9 cm.

We Do — Together

CM = 7 units. M is the midpoint of CD. What is MD?

Show guided answer

MD = 7 units because the two halves are equal.

Algebra extension

AM = 2x + 5 and MB = 17. Set the equal parts equal: 2x + 5 = 17 → 2x = 12 → x = 6.

You Do + 10-question exit ticket

Questions 1–6 are independent practice. Questions 7–10 complete the exit ticket. Select one answer per question, then check your work. You can retry.

Privacy: answers are checked in this browser only. This page does not collect names or send scores to a teacher dashboard.

Teacher accommodations

Provide extra processing time, read-aloud directions, one problem at a time, a two-rule reference card, and guided equation templates. Keep grade-level geometry terminology while reducing unnecessary language complexity.
