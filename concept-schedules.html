Report individual course grades first. If the school adopts an overall progress average, calculate it as ELA × .25 + Math × .25 + Science × .15 + Social Studies × .15 + Health/PE × .10 + Studio × .10. For example, course scores of 80, 90, 85, 80, 95 and 90 produce 85.75. Do not use that combined number as the sole promotion decision or to conceal a student’s reading or mathematics needs.

Course level: All courses in this mock plan are regular, unweighted middle-school courses, with no honors bonus. These schedules do not award high-school Carnegie units or establish a high-school graduation pathway. Any future Algebra I credit offering needs a separately verified course and credit plan.

One studio period, five weekly experiences

Both grades receive a 35-minute studio each day. Over a normal five-day week that is 175 minutes total, not 175 minutes for every elective. Each strand receives 35 scheduled minutes per week. Longer cooking or art projects can use a documented rotation with equivalent time across the cycle.

Proposed grading within each class

This draft uses several ways to show knowledge. An accommodation changes access without automatically changing the learning target. If a target is modified, record that clearly.

Four quarters would contribute 25% each to the annual course grade. A draft ten-point reporting scale is A 90–100, B 80–89, C 70–79, D 60–69 and F below 60. These are proposed school policies, not a claim that every middle-school private course must use this scale. Studio reports should identify each strand so a combined mark does not hide different strengths.

Keep growth and access visible. Report individualized goal progress separately from course mastery; an IEP goal is not an extra graded subject. Attendance, compliance, eye contact, speech ability, speed, disability-related needs, and family help with homework should not substitute for evidence of learning. Provide accessible reassessment after reteaching.

Capoeira / Health & PE: use adapted skill demonstrations, music and cultural understanding, health knowledge, and reflection. Assess each learner’s documented targets and safe choices, not athletic talent, contact, inversions, or ability to perform the same movement as another student. Seated and non-movement roles remain meaningful options.

External assessment: this concept assigns LEAP or another annual external assessment 0% of routine course grades. That is a proposed local choice, subject to any applicable program or course rules; it does not excuse a required assessment.

Individual support and a workable school day

Keep the planned group size of up to seven students with an educator and paraprofessional. Use visual schedules, first / then supports, AAC, assistive technology, chunked tasks, and individualized breaks. The 30-minute literacy lab is targeted instruction in reading or writing, not an unstructured study hall. Broader services and goal work must be scheduled from the actual student plan.

Academic and life-skills emphases may use the same timetable with different tasks and documented supports. A disability label does not automatically replace core subjects, change assessment eligibility, or make a student eligible for an alternate assessment. Additional service time must be placed deliberately, with missed instruction addressed.

Arrival, transitions, lunch, and the scheduled sensory break contribute zero instructional minutes in this model. Unplanned interruptions and additional breaks must be recorded accurately and addressed in the final calendar. Do not count the same period twice or assume that an activity becomes instructional merely by renaming it.

Calendar and staffing feasibility

A planning year of 180 full instructional days at 345 instructional minutes gives 62,100 scheduled instructional minutes. An actual calendar must deduct holidays, closures, shortened days, and other lost instruction. Staff-only workdays are separate.

Bulletin 741 Nonpublic §117 sets an annual instructional-time requirement of 57,750 minutes and excludes lunch, recess and class changes. Section 2103 requires approved nonpublic grades 1–8 to devote at least half the school day to reading, language arts and mathematics. This concept reserves 210 of 420 minutes for those subjects. Source: BESE Bulletin 741 Nonpublic, §§117 and 2103 (reviewed November 13, 2026).

The difference between 62,100 and 57,750 is 4,350 minutes; this is a planning margin, not permission to cancel instruction or a determination that all attendance and calendar rules are satisfied.

These are student templates, not a completed staffing master schedule. Four groups of seven would require 120 group-minutes of Capoeira instruction per day if taught separately. Ten groups would require 300; twenty groups across both grades would require 600. One specialist cannot deliver 600 minutes within this student day. The opening plan must fund adequate qualified instructors, planning time, support, safe spaces and staggered sessions; it must not quietly combine groups beyond the seven-student commitment.

Must Aberewa students take LEAP?

Not automatically just because the school is in Louisiana. The applicable requirement depends on the school’s actual category, the student’s funding / placement arrangement, and current program rules. No school category or testing program is adopted by this concept page.

Disability and testing: follow the selected assessment’s accommodation rules and the student’s applicable documentation. LA GATOR law preserves exemptions that would apply to the student in public school. Autism, SLD, an IEP, or placement in a life-skills group does not by itself establish an exemption or alternate-assessment eligibility.

Proposed planning sequence: confirm the approval and funding model; identify covered students; choose the permitted assessment; budget administration and staff training; verify accommodations and test security; publish a family testing calendar; report results through the required process. Use classroom evidence and growth information alongside the external results.

Capoeira is part of the whole day

The daily Health & PE block combines progressive movement, safe practice, music and health instruction; it is not thirty minutes of continuous exertion. Cultural connections also appear in reading, history, patterns, rhythm and art. Capoeira remains the only planned school sport, inspired by Mestre Curtis Pierre’s published approach. Every depicted participant wears the same Aberewa emblem uniform and athletic shoes. Free adult classes and guest visits are separate from student instructional minutes.
